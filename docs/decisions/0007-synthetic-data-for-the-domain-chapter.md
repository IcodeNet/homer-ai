# 0007. Invented, generated data for the company examples of chapter 10

Status: Accepted

## Context

Chapter 10 takes the course's pattern (your data, a tool, an MCP server, a score with and without) to the kind of work a company does: a support assistant for a savings firm, a classifier for complaints and vulnerable customers in email, and triage for an IT service desk. Each example needs documents, labelled messages and a test set.

Real data of that kind can't go into a public repository. Customer emails and support tickets hold personal detail, and stripping the names doesn't make them safe: a message about an illness or a death still points at a person. A real firm's product sheets and rates belong to that firm, and a course that quoted them would look as if it spoke for it. Public datasets of this kind exist for some tasks, but they would add downloads and licences, and none covers the three examples with one consistent, small story.

The course also has to run on a laptop in short sessions, and CI must be able to check everything that doesn't need GPT-2.

## Decision

- All data of chapter 10 is invented and lives in `data/domain/`, under the project's MIT licence. Its README says so first, and so does the head of every product sheet and article. The firm is called "Example Bank". No real company, product, person or customer appears.
- The small, structured parts are written by hand: 4 product sheets and a FAQ (39 sections, each sheet with a version and an in-force date), a rates table, 39 labelled support questions, 10 knowledge-base articles, and 24 handwritten emails.
- The larger sets are generated by `scripts/make_domain_data.py` from lists of sentences with a seeded random number generator: 400 emails and 200 tickets. The same seed gives the same bytes. The output is committed, and `tests/test_domain.py` checks that the committed files are what the script writes.
- The generator splits **by wording**: about a quarter of the sentences in each list are used for the test set only. A random split of the finished texts would put near-copies of the training texts into the test set.
- The sentence lists include cases a keyword list gets wrong on purpose: complaints and signs of vulnerability with no trigger word, and trigger words in messages that are neither.
- The 24 handwritten emails are kept apart from the templates and used only as a reality check in notebook 10_02.
- Rules such as the UK's Consumer Duty and complaint-handling rules are mentioned only loosely, as the reason for a design. The invented limits and timescales are not taken from any rule, and nothing in the chapter claims that a system complies with one.

## Consequences

- Nothing personal and nothing owned by a firm is in the repository, and nobody needs an account or a download for the data.
- The chapter's numbers are repeatable, and the ones that need no model are pinned by tests: recall@k of the support search, the word-list guard, the keyword baseline, the article suggestions and the three ticket routers.
- Template text is much easier than real text, and the chapter says so wherever a score appears. Notebook 10_02 measures the gap: on the 24 handwritten emails the keyword list caught 0 of 12 complaints (20 of 39 on the templates), and the fine-tuned model's recall on vulnerability fell from 0.91 to 0.58.
- The way the data is split changes the scores a great deal. Notebook 10_03 shows the same ticket router at 30 of 50 on unseen wordings and 42 of 50 on a random split.
- The labels are one author's judgement. In a real project the people who own the process label the data, and disagree on the hard cases.
- The sets are small (80 test emails, 50 test tickets, 39 support questions), so one case moves a score by 1 to 3 points. Notebook 10_04 prints the ranges.
- Changing a sentence list changes the generated files, the pinned numbers in `tests/test_domain.py`, and the tables in `scripts/course/ch10_your_domain.py`. [CONTRIBUTING.md](../../CONTRIBUTING.md) lists the steps.
